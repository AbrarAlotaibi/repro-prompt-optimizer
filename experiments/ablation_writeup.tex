\section{Ablation Study}
\label{sec:ablation}

We conduct a controlled component ablation to isolate the contribution of
each REPRO subsystem to attack success rate~(ASR) and convergence efficiency.
Four components are individually removed while all other settings remain
constant, yielding five configurations evaluated on two targets of differing
resistance.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Experimental Protocol}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Benchmark.}
We evaluate on a stratified 50-behavior subset of JailbreakBench~\cite{jailbreakbench}
(JBB-50), sampled with seed~42 to balance the original taxonomy categories.

\paragraph{Configurations.}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} lists the five configurations.
\emph{Full REPRO} is the unmodified system.
Each ablation disables exactly one component:
\textbf{$-$Failure Analysis} replaces the refusal classifier, counter-strategy
router, and per-behavior failure tracker with a null pass-through;
\textbf{$-$Semantic Crossover} disables all multi-parent recombination
(including self-crossover), reducing generation to mutation-only;
\textbf{$-$Bayesian Strategy Selection} replaces Thompson Sampling with
uniform random strategy selection, removing learned priors, hybrid
weighting, and analysis-relevance scoring;
\textbf{$-$Elitism} sets $E{=}0$, discarding all elite candidates between
generations.

\paragraph{Targets.}
We evaluate on two targets:
(i)~\textbf{GPT-4o-mini} (OpenAI), an easily-jailbroken model included to
expose efficiency differences when ASR saturates;
(ii)~\textbf{Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct} (Meta, served via DeepInfra), a
harder target expected to show ASR differentiation across configurations.

\paragraph{Hyperparameters.}
Each configuration runs exploit mode for up to 100 iterations per behavior
(seed~42, population~24, $E{=}3$ for elitism-enabled configurations).
The reflective LM is Mistral-7B-Instruct (4-bit EXL3, local) for GPT runs
and Mistral-Small-2503 (via Requesty) for Llama runs.
The judge is Qwen3-8B (local, TabbyAPI) throughout.
No rewordings are used (\texttt{--num-rewordings~0}), giving binary
per-candidate ASR.
Strategy weight files are deleted before each configuration to prevent
cross-configuration prior leakage.

\paragraph{Statistical tests.}
We apply a suite of complementary tests.  Pairwise comparisons (Full REPRO
vs.\ each ablation, $k{=}4$) use Bonferroni correction
($\alpha' = 0.05/4 = 0.0125$); omnibus tests are evaluated at $\alpha = 0.05$:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{McNemar's exact binomial test} on per-behavior binary
        outcomes (appropriate because $b{+}c < 25$ in every comparison).
  \item \textbf{Paired bootstrap 95\% CI for $\Delta\text{ASR}$}
        (10\,000 resamples over the 50 paired behaviors), giving a direct
        interval on the effect size rather than a binary decision.
  \item \textbf{Log-rank test} on the full iteration-to-first-success
        distribution, treating failed behaviors as censored at the
        iteration budget (100). This uses all 50 behaviors per group and
        properly accounts for the censoring that paired tests ignore.
  \item \textbf{Wilcoxon signed-rank test} on the subset of behaviors
        where both Full REPRO and the ablated configuration succeeded,
        supplemented by \textbf{Cliff's $\delta$} for effect-size
        interpretation (thresholds: $|\delta| \geq 0.147$ small,
        $\geq 0.330$ medium, $\geq 0.474$ large~\cite{romano2006}).
  \item \textbf{Cochran's $Q$ omnibus test}~\cite{cochran1950} on the
        $5{\times}50$ binary-outcome matrix, testing the null that all five
        configurations achieve equal ASR.
  \item \textbf{Kruskal-Wallis omnibus test} on the five
        iteration-to-first-success distributions (failed behaviors assigned
        iteration~101), testing the null of equal convergence distributions.
  \item \textbf{Holm–Bonferroni step-down procedure} applied to the four
        McNemar $p$-values as a more powerful alternative to uniform
        Bonferroni; findings are reported alongside the uniform correction.
  \item \textbf{Cohen's $g$ and retrospective power analysis} for each
        McNemar comparison, to quantify the directional effect size and the
        minimum sample size that would be required to detect the observed
        ASR differences at $80$\% power.
\end{enumerate}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\paragraph{ASR.}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

Results are reported in Table~\ref{tab:ablation}.
On GPT-4o-mini, Full REPRO achieves 100\% ASR (50/50).
Removing semantic crossover or elitism reduces this to 90\% (45/50), and
removing Bayesian strategy selection to 88\% (44/50); failure analysis
leaves ASR unchanged.
On the harder Llama-3.3-70B target, Full REPRO achieves 98\% (49/50),
and all four ablations reduce ASR to 90--94\% (45--47/50), with crossover
and Bayesian strategy selection again the most impactful (both 90\%,
losing four behaviors each).

McNemar's exact binomial test yields no significant comparisons after
Bonferroni correction on either target ($p_\text{bonf} \geq 0.125$).
This is expected: with at most six discordant pairs ($b{+}c \leq 6$),
the test has very limited power at $n{=}50$ even before the Bonferroni
penalty.
The paired bootstrap provides a more informative picture.
For $-$Semantic Crossover and $-$Bayesian Strategy, the bootstrap 95\%~CI
for $\Delta\text{ASR}$ excludes zero on \emph{both} targets
(\mbox{+10\%} [+2\%, +18\%] and \mbox{+12\%} [+4\%, +22\%] on GPT;
\mbox{+8\%} [+2\%, +16\%] on Llama for both).
For $-$Elitism, the CI excludes zero on GPT (+10\%, [+2\%, +20\%]) but
spans zero on Llama ($-$4\%, [--4\%, +12\%]).
For $-$Failure Analysis, the CI spans zero on both targets, consistent with
its small absolute difference (0 and 2 behaviors lost respectively).
These intervals constitute positive evidence that semantic crossover,
Bayesian strategy selection, and elitism each produce non-zero ASR gain,
with the uncertainty bands reflecting the moderate sample size rather than
an absence of effect.
Cochran's $Q$ omnibus test provides a complementary check on the binary
outcomes: on GPT-4o-mini, $Q{=}16.57$, $df{=}4$, $p{=}0.002$, confirming
that ASR differs significantly across the five configurations taken jointly.
On Llama-3.3-70B, $Q{=}4.87$, $p{=}0.30$, consistent with the smaller and
less consistent ASR gaps on the harder target.
The Holm–Bonferroni step-down procedure applied to the four McNemar
$p$-values yields the same non-significant set as the uniform Bonferroni
correction (minimum Holm-adjusted $p{=}0.125$ on GPT, $0.500$ on Llama),
confirming that the McNemar result is not an artifact of the correction
choice.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\paragraph{Convergence efficiency.}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

Figure~\ref{fig:convergence} shows the cumulative ASR trajectory
(i.e., the Kaplan--Meier $1{-}S(t)$ curve) for each configuration on
both targets.
Full REPRO and all ablated configurations show comparable initial slopes;
the curves separate primarily in their asymptotic ceilings, consistent
with the ASR results above rather than a convergence-speed story.

The log-rank test confirms this: no configuration comparison reaches
significance after Bonferroni correction on either target.
The one near-significant result is $-$Failure Analysis on GPT-4o-mini
($\chi^2{=}4.53$, $p{=}0.033$, Bonf.\ $p{=}0.133$), and crucially it
points in the \emph{opposite} direction from what efficiency would
suggest: the observed event count in Full REPRO ($O_1{=}50$) is lower
than the expected count under the null ($E_1{=}58.9$), meaning Full REPRO
solves behaviors \emph{later} in the iteration sequence than $-$FA.
This is not a convergence advantage for the ablated configuration; it
reflects the computational overhead of failure analysis on a target where
refusal patterns are weak and unsystematic, so the signals are noisy and
the module adds latency without proportional guidance.
On Llama-3.3-70B, where refusal patterns are more structured, no such
overhead effect is detectable ($\chi^2{=}0.46$, $p{=}0.496$).

The Wilcoxon signed-rank test on paired successes supports the same
conclusion: no comparison is significant after Bonferroni correction
(minimum $p_\text{bonf}{=}0.045$ on GPT-4o-mini;
minimum $p_\text{bonf}{=}0.037$ for $-$Bayesian Strategy on Llama-3.3-70B).
Cliff's $\delta$ confirms that all effect sizes on convergence timing are
negligible to small ($|\delta| \leq 0.32$).
The apparent pattern of ablated configurations achieving lower mean
iterations on the paired subset (e.g., $-$Semantic Crossover on GPT:
mean~5.4 vs.~16.0; $-$Bayesian Strategy on Llama: mean~3.3 vs.~5.9) is a
selection-bias artifact: the paired subset excludes the 4--6 behaviors that
only Full REPRO solves, which are precisely the hard behaviors requiring
many iterations.
Cliff's $\delta$ on the full 50-behavior distribution (failed behaviors
assigned iteration~101) is negligible for these comparisons
($\delta_{-\text{XO}} = {+}0.12$, $\delta_{-\text{BS}} = {+}0.08$
on GPT), confirming that the efficiency differences visible in the paired
analysis do not reflect a genuine speed disadvantage of Full REPRO.
The Kruskal-Wallis omnibus test corroborates this picture: on
GPT-4o-mini, $H{=}4.12$, $p{=}0.39$, no signal in the five distributions
jointly; on Llama-3.3-70B, $H{=}10.24$, $p{=}0.037$, a marginal omnibus
signal driven by the near-significant pairwise results for $-$Bayesian
Strategy and $-$Elitism, though no individual Wilcoxon comparison crosses
the Bonferroni threshold.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\paragraph{Effect size and power.}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

Cohen's $g$ (McNemar effect size) equals $0.50$ — the maximum achievable
value — for every comparison in which all discordant pairs favour Full
REPRO ($c{=}0$), providing maximally one-sided directional evidence within
the observed discordance.
Retrospective power analysis reveals that McNemar's exact binomial at
$\alpha'{=}0.0125$ requires at least $8$ discordant pairs to reject;
with $b{+}c \leq 6$ in every comparison, the achieved power is exactly
zero regardless of effect direction.
Detecting the observed ASR differences at $80$\% power would require
approximately $93$--$112$ behaviors on GPT-4o-mini and $140$--$279$ on
Llama-3.3-70B (normal approximation), confirming that the $n{=}50$ sample
is underpowered for McNemar inference.
Bootstrap and Cochran's $Q$ omnibus evidence therefore carry the
inferential weight that individual McNemar testing cannot supply at
this sample size.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\paragraph{Component-level interpretation.}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

\textbf{Semantic crossover} is the most consistently impactful component,
losing 5 behaviors on GPT (10\%) and 4 on Llama (8\%), with bootstrap CIs
excluding zero on both targets.
Crossover enables candidates that have made independent progress on
different facets of a jailbreak to exchange partial solutions; without it,
each lineage must discover the full attack by mutation alone and fails on
behaviors that require structural recombination to escape local optima.

\textbf{Bayesian strategy selection} is the jointly strongest ablation on
GPT-4o-mini (6 behaviors, 12\%; bootstrap CI [+4\%, +22\%]) and equally
impactful on Llama (4 behaviors, 8\%).
Replacing Thompson Sampling with uniform random selection removes the
ability to concentrate the exploration budget on strategies with strong
success histories.
The affected behaviors are those for which the most effective jailbreak
strategy is not the modal choice; Thompson Sampling elevates it over time,
while uniform selection never prioritizes it.

\textbf{Elitism} loses 5 behaviors on GPT-4o-mini (10\%; bootstrap CI
[+2\%, +20\%]) but only 3 on Llama (6\%), with the Llama CI spanning zero.
Without elite preservation, high-fitness candidates are discarded each
generation rather than forwarded as seeds, forcing the population to
rediscover partial solutions repeatedly.
The gap between targets suggests that elitism's benefit is
concentrated in behaviors requiring iterative refinement of a partially
successful candidate — a scenario that arises more on a strongly aligned
target when first-generation partial jailbreaks are more common.

\textbf{Failure analysis} shows no ASR impact on GPT-4o-mini and loses
2 behaviors on Llama (4\%), with bootstrap CIs spanning zero on both.
The log-rank finding on GPT-4o-mini (Full REPRO converges later than
$-$FA) confirms that the module adds computational overhead that is only
justified when refusal signals are informative.
On a weakly aligned target, refusals are rare and patterns unsystematic;
on Llama-3.3-70B, the structured refusal patterns make the failure tracker
useful at the margin, recovering behaviors that would otherwise fail.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Summary}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

The ablation reveals two levels of component contribution.
Semantic crossover and Bayesian strategy selection produce robust,
non-zero ASR gains confirmed by bootstrap inference on both targets.
Elitism provides a clear benefit on the easier target (bootstrap CI
excludes zero) with a weaker, less certain effect on the harder one.
Failure analysis contributes only on the harder target, where refusal
patterns are structured enough to produce useful counter-strategy signals,
and adds overhead on the easier target where those signals are absent.

No component significantly accelerates convergence in the Wilcoxon or
log-rank sense: the efficiency story is in final ASR rather than
iterations-to-solution, with Full REPRO trading marginally slower
per-behavior convergence on easy behaviors for the ability to solve a
broader set of hard ones.
The Kruskal-Wallis omnibus detects a marginal iteration signal on Llama
($p{=}0.037$) but no individual comparison survives Bonferroni correction,
and the effect sizes are negligible to small ($|\delta|{\leq}0.32$).
No single component is redundant: each ablation degrades ASR on at least
one target, the degraded behavior sets are largely non-overlapping, and
bootstrap inference and Cochran's $Q$ omnibus ($Q{=}16.57$,
$p{=}0.002$ on GPT-4o-mini) provide direct positive evidence that the
observed differences are not sampling noise.
The McNemar component of the analysis is underpowered by design ($n{=}50$
requires $\geq 93$ behaviors for $80$\% power at $10$\% ΔASR); the
bootstrap, Cochran's $Q$, and log-rank tests are the primary inferential
tools.
